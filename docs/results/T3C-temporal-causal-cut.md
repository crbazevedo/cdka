# T3.C / T4.B — A finite temporal information cut that defeats marginal feasibility

**Status (2026-11-01):** an exactly solved counterexample and a general finite-decision lemma, both applications of **known** binary testing, total-variation contraction, Blackwell ordering, set/observation compatibility, and finite-state controller synthesis. This is **not** an original CDKA theorem.

## 1. Target and backward specification

We seek a finite physical system in which:
1. **Sensing marginal:** an actual sensor can distinguish the relevant hidden states with one bit.
2. **Actuation marginal:** if supplied the correct state at the decision instant, a repaired actuator can execute every required intervention perfectly.
3. **Repair/resource marginal:** the available resource budget suffices to restore actuator operation by the deadline.
4. **Joint impossibility:** no causally implementable policy using the complete physical state **actually available at that deadline** achieves the required viability probability.

The three marginal certificates are necessary but not sufficient for a *common* controller: their witnessing policies/observations occur at different times. To avoid a trivial resource-shortfall construction, the base example spends **one** repair token, exactly the available budget. The obstruction is destruction of the information needed to select the repaired actuator's action.

Important: these are only **separately formulated marginal checks**, not a correctly solved POMDP or a fully sequenced information constraint. A complete finite-state controller analysis detects the failure.

## 2. The smallest deterministic witness in the selected architecture

- World W∈{0,1}, initially uniform and static.
- Two incompatible terminal actions A={0,1}; viability requires U=W.
- Initially the sensor is operational and reads W perfectly, while the actuator is unavailable. The observation is stored in a **volatile physical register** M∈{0,1}.
- Exactly one repair of cost 1 is compulsory before terminal actuation; the resource budget is B=1.
- The repair makes both actuator commands available, disables further sensing and resets the same physical register to the constant M=0.
- At decision time the **only** physically accessible state carrying possible information about W is M. Repair choices, clocks, resources, actuator configuration and other signals are fixed and cannot encode W. No free policy recall or external transcript is allowed.

Before repair, I(W;M)=1 bit. After the compulsory reset, I(W;M')=0, although the actuator has full physical authority and the repair resource was sufficient.

For every physically implementable terminal decision rule π:M'→{0,1}, P(π(M')=W)=1/2. Randomizing does not improve the optimum. An omniscient terminal oracle would succeed with probability 1. A one-bit nonvolatile register that survives repair restores success to 1.

This is a counterexample to the claim that **marginal observability + marginal actuation + marginal repair-resource feasibility imply jointly achievable viability**.

### Restrained minimality claim

Within the family of deterministic **binary** sensor and repair maps, injectivity of the sensor is needed for its information marginal; noninjectivity of the compulsory repair is needed to destroy the distinct bits. There are exactly two injective maps {0,1}→{0,1} and two noninjective (constant) maps, hence **four** minimal witnesses. Our enumerator checks all four. We do not claim a global minimum over arbitrary automata, protocols or physical organizations.

## 3. The correct theorem uses a complete causal information cut

Let C be the **entire physically accessible pre-decision state**, potentially including memory M, organization Z, resources R, residual observations, and physically readable traces of previous actions. The two current worlds W∈{0,1} have equal priors and require opposite actions. Both actions are feasible at the terminal instant.

**Binary causal-cut lemma (classical).**

\[
\sup_{\pi:C\to\{0,1\}} P(\pi(C)=W)
=\frac{1+\mathrm{TV}(P(C|W=0),P(C|W=1))}{2}.
\]

**Proof:** for D={c:π(c)=1}, success is
\[
\frac12[P(C\notin D|W=0)+P(C\in D|W=1)]
=\frac12[1+P_1(D)-P_0(D)].
\]
The supremum over D is total variation. Randomized decoders cannot exceed the convex extreme. QED.

This conditional law must be the one generated by the **actual physically admissible pre-decision policy**. If a policy can change what C contains, one must optimize over such policies, not freeze an artificially narrow register. Also, if terminal action availability or effectiveness depends on W or C, the equality can become only a bound and a general policy synthesis problem is required.

**Finite multiclass support theorem (elementary).** For safe actions A_w and finite reachable signals c with possible-world support S(c)={w:P(C=c|W=w)>0}, a deterministic zero-error decoder exists exactly when

\[
\forall c\text{ reachable},\quad \bigcap_{w\in S(c)} A_w\ne\varnothing.
\]

The proof is selecting any common safe action for every reachable c; if there is no common action, all choices fail in some compatible world. This combines the earlier Dowker compatibility structure with the physically **realized** memory kernel, rather than a freely designed code.

## 4. Exact finite-memory degradation and repair-resource theorem

We now let:
- η∈[0,1/2] be the initial symmetric sensor classification error;
- ρ∈[0,1] be the erasure probability in each *unprotected* repair;
- ν∈[0,1/2] be the chance that the physical world flips independently during each repair;
- n≥1 be the number of mandatory repairs before terminal action;
- B≥0 be the integer resource budget; each repair costs 1 and protecting a repair against **memory erasure** costs one additional token.

**Restricted architecture:** the repair schedule is precommitted before knowing W; its choices or resource accounting are **not** physically readable at the terminal deadline as an implicit memory channel. No new observation occurs after the initial sensor reading. A repair protection does not prevent a physical flip of W. All random channels are independent conditional on their declared parent variables. The final actuator is available iff B≥n.

Let
\[
k^*(n,B)=\min(n,B-n)\quad(B\ge n)
\]
be the maximum number of protected repairs. Then the optimal terminal viability in this architecture is

\[
\boxed{
P^*_{\rm viable}(n,B)=
\begin{cases}
0,&B<n,\\[2mm]
\frac12\left[1+(1-2\eta)(1-2\nu)^n
(1-\rho)^{n-k^*}\right],&B\ge n.
\end{cases}
}
\]

**Derivation:** the sensor begins with binary correlation 1−2η. Each independent physical state flip multiplies correlation by 1−2ν. Each unprotected erasure transmits the surviving bit with probability 1−ρ and otherwise produces an explicit erasure symbol, contributing no information about a uniform terminal W. Protected repairs preserve the bit. The terminal binary hypothesis-testing formula yields the result; protecting more repairs weakly improves correlation, so the budget is optimally spent on as many protections as allowed. This also proves sufficiency through the corresponding likelihood decoder. QED under model assumptions.

**Mutual information at the decision deadline:**

Let p_keep=(1−ρ)^(n−k*) and η_n=[1−(1−2η)(1−2ν)^n]/2. Then
\[
I(W_{\rm final};M_{\rm final})
=p_{\rm keep}[1-h_2(\eta_n)]
\]
for B≥n. One bit measured at time zero is not automatically one usable bit after maintenance.

### Hard-reset phase transition

If η=ν=0 and ρ=1, then:
- B<n: no actuator at deadline → success 0.
- n≤B<2n: at least one compulsory unprotected repair erases all usable knowledge → success 1/2.
- B≥2n: every repair can protect the register → success 1.

This is an exact integer-budget, finite-horizon **organizational information threshold**. It is an example, not a universal phase transition.

For 0<ρ<1, and no protection or world drift (η=ν=0, B=n), one gets
\[
P^*=\tfrac12[1+(1-\rho)^n]\longrightarrow1/2.
\]
Repeated necessary maintenance erodes control information even while the actuator remains functional. If ν>0, repairing memory alone cannot prevent the loss of correctness due to changes in the external state.

### Deadline/resource feasibility certificate

For desired failure risk α<1/2 and B≥n, viability is achievable iff
\[
(1-2\eta)(1-2\nu)^n(1-\rho)^{n-k^*}\ge1-2\alpha.
\]
This condition identifies exactly which information bottleneck or additional resource prevents achieving a specified reliability.

## 5. An adversarial counterexample to the memory-only cut

Suppose repairing still resets volatile M to 0, but a **pre-repair physical action** configures a persistent, observable actuator mode Z=W. At the terminal instant the control mechanism can read Z and execute U=Z.

Then
\[
I(W;M_{\rm final})=0,\qquad
I(W;M_{\rm final},Z_{\rm final})=1\text{ bit},
\]
and success is 1. The alleged no-go disappears.

Therefore, CDKA must not locate knowledge in an arbitrarily selected register. **The complete observable physical organization may itself serve as memory.** Actions can create information-bearing states; repairs may destroy or preserve these states.

This is closely related to nonclassical information structures and signaling through actions in decentralized control. It is a *required falsification test* for any proposed universal memory or autonomy theorem.

## 6. Decomposition and exact verification

- **Backward specification:** incompatible safe actions → required distinction → physically accessible terminal cut → channel history → compulsory repair transitions → resource authorization.
- **Boolean support analysis:** zero-error compatibility via intersection of admissible actions over possible worlds for each terminal signal.
- **Probabilistic cut:** exact terminal P(W,C) via finite Markov-kernel propagation; upper bound and attainment via TV/Bayes classification.
- **Resource search:** enumerate every nonadaptive protected/unprotected repair schedule satisfying n+k≤B; compare with closed form.
- **Minimal deterministic search:** enumerate all 2^2 sensor maps ×2^2 repair maps and identify the four injective-sensor/noninjective-repair obstructions.
- **Adversarial organizational bypass:** introduce a persistent Z signal and verify restored viability.

## 7. What this does NOT prove

- No original mathematical theorem: Bayes binary testing, contraction, finite-state controllers and viability are established fields.
- No physical energy-to-information conversion; tokens are an explicit discrete accounting model.
- No spontaneous emergence of goals or agency; we impose the actuator's required action and a viability criterion.
- No proof of a general impossibility when a controller has unrestricted history, observable protection decisions, external stores, or communication through physical configuration. These channels must be included in C.
- No claim that information, action and repair cannot ever be simultaneously feasible. **They can** if their physical channels and budgets permit it.

## 8. The actual frontier

Replace the precommitted repair plan with a policy that can:
1. choose repairs and channel protections based on its **physically realized** knowledge;
2. alter its own accessible physical memory channels (including Z,R,X), perhaps externalizing information into the environment;
3. retain or recover actuation viability under repeated shocks; and
4. trade off future sensing, physical control and autonomous repair under the same material constraints.

Find a sufficient statistic and necessary/sufficient conditions on the **complete causal cut** for recurrent viability. Compare to constrained and decentralized POMDPs, finite-state controllers, nonclassical information patterns, and stochastic viability before making originality claims.
