# CDKA operational ontology — draft v0.1

## Scientific scope

The object is a **physical organization**, not an abstract optimizer. In the first finite model its state is
\[
S_t=(X_t,Z_t,R_t,M_t),
\]
where X is the regulated environment, Z is material organization (including sensors/actuators), R is available resource state, and M is internal memory. The observation law is O_Z(y|S), the physically available action set is A(Z,R), and the world transition kernel is P(S'|S,u).

Crucially, an experimental `do(u)` is an intervention by an investigator; a naturally occurring U is generated by the physical organization. An externally imposed controller must not be silently counted as autonomous.

## Distinct primitives

| Term | Operational interpretation | What it does **not** imply |
|---|---|---|
| Physical possibility | Belongs to the support of some admissible trajectory law at declared horizon and initial state | Probability or knowledge |
| Predictability | Small uncertainty about an outcome conditional on specified information | Influence |
| Influence | Two realizable interventions produce different laws of consequences | Sensing or skill |
| Potential control | Some admissible intervention policy reaches/sustains a target set | That this controller can select it with current information |
| Informed control | Existing sensing/memory and deadlines permit selection of successful interventions | Self-maintenance |
| Decision sufficiency | A code retains enough distinctions to select *some* admissible/safe action | Counterfactual truth for all alternative actions |
| Counterfactual knowledge | Physically realized representation accurately distinguishes relevant interventional consequences | Causal utilization of the representation |
| Operational use | Intervening on the representation changes action selection and downstream outcomes under controls | Truth of the representation |
| Viability | Continued operation of explicitly identified constitutive processes for a declared horizon | Universal purpose |
| Operational autonomy | Internal organization contributes causally to continued sensing, action and maintenance under disturbances | Isolation, consciousness, or absolute agency |
| Minimal agency | Candidate conjunction of individuation, internally contingent intervention and self-maintenance | A proven universal agency criterion |

## Trajectory sets and epistemic scope

\[
\mathcal C_T(s)=\{P_s^\pi(S_{0:T}\in\cdot): \pi\in\Pi_{\rm phys}(s)\}.
\]

Here `Pi_phys` must state exactly which feedback observations, interventions, delays and budgets are physically realizable. An omniscient-policy benchmark has a *different* policy class; do not conflate it with the organization itself.

For histories h,h', a candidate predictive equivalence relation is
\[
h\sim_\Pi h'\iff \forall\pi\in\Pi,\quad K_T(h,\pi)=K_T(h',\pi),
\]
with future-intervention conditional laws K. This is task-, horizon-, model-, and policy-class-relative. It is not an absolute definition of knowledge.

## Constitutive boundaries and causal validation

A viability set V must be stated with its constitutive rationale, physical measurements, perturbations and time horizon. Do not infer intention from persistence alone. Compare:
- passive attractor vs active feedback;
- sensorless actuation vs informed response;
- predictive but disconnected model vs utilized memory;
- matched external replay vs endogenous feedback;
- externally repaired system vs endogenous repair.

Interpretations remain **candidates**, not ontological proofs. See [hypotheses](../registry/hypotheses.md).
