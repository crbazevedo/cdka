# Counterfactual Dynamics of Knowledge and Agency (CDKA)

**Research notebook v0.1 — 2026-11-01**  
**Status:** exploratory program, not a manuscript or claim of novelty.  
**Working thesis:** Knowledge is a relational property of an internal representation, a family of physically admissible interventions, and their counterfactual trajectory laws. Autonomy concerns the endogenous preservation and regeneration of the mechanisms that make such a relation possible.

## 0. What CDKA is (and is not)

**Scientific problem.** Give non-circular mathematical criteria for how coupled physical dynamics can support (i) potential causal influence, (ii) epistemic access to intervention outcomes, (iii) effective regulation, (iv) maintenance of an organization that sustains its own interventions, and (v) operational agency, without postulating a universal externally assigned reward or teleological force.

**Not claimed:** a discovery of control rate–distortion, semantic information based on viability, causal states, or informational empowerment. All have strong precedents. Nor is the stationary-action principle a universal minimization law or evidence that matter 'wants' anything.

**Target novelty (unverified):** a compositional theory of *endogenously evolving* controlled trajectory spaces, admissible interventions, operationally meaningful representations, viable organizational regimes, and the information rates necessary for robust persistence as the physical constraints themselves change.

## 1. Prior-art ledger and novelty guardrails

### Established or very close to established

1. **Polani et al. (2006):** relevant information as the minimum state–action information for specified expected decision utility. This already has a rate–distortion structure.
2. **Fox & Tishby (2016), Parts I–II:** memoryless and retentive minimum-information LQG controllers, sequential rate–distortion under performance constraints. This is a very close mathematical antecedent of `inf I(H;M)` given a performance loss.
3. **Kolchinsky & Wolpert (2018):** semantic information as counterfactually viability-relevant correlations; intervention can scramble correlations to test their causal necessity.
4. **Crutchfield/Barnett (2015/2016):** causal-state and epsilon-transducer representations sufficient to model input–output processes; controlled future equivalence classes have precedents.
5. **Klyubin, Polani & Nehaniv (2005, 2008):** empowerment and an information-theoretic account of options observable through the perception–action loop.
6. **Wissner-Gross & Freer (2013):** causal-entropic forces: a particular dynamical preference for entropy of accessible future paths, not a general theory of knowledge or autonomy.
7. **Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde (2009):** self-individuation, interactional asymmetry and normativity; agency as organization regulating its environmental coupling while sustaining itself.
8. **Beni (first published 2025):** agency as the functional capacity to build counterfactual models of action outcomes. A close philosophical predecessor of our proposed causal-knowledge criterion.
9. **Murray (SSRN preprint, September 2026, not independently peer-reviewed here):** 'From Predictive State to Viable Action.' Viable-action state abstractions have a set-cover / hypergraph characterization, exactly anticipating a static construction that might otherwise appear novel in CDKA.
10. **Networked-control data-rate theorems:** lower bounds on information rates required for stabilization are already well established under specified assumptions.
11. **Freidlin–Wentzell, Boué–Dupuis, Girsanov, Gibbs variational principle, Schrödinger bridges:** established links between stochastic path actions, relative entropy and intervention/control effort.
12. **Georgiev (2025 online; April 2026 print):** a proposed stochastic–dissipative least-action framework for biological self-organization, deserving methodological comparison; its generality should not be assumed.

### Claims not to make without a literature review and theorem

- 'We invented rate–distortion for knowledge/control.' No.
- 'We invented viable-action sufficient representations.' No.
- 'Persistence means agency.' No.
- 'Entropy-maximizing trajectories create intelligence in general.' No.
- 'All coupled physical subsystems minimize independent actions.' No.
- 'Noncomputability or chaos is necessary or sufficient for agency.' No.

## 2. Mathematical primitives: physical dynamics first

Take a physical system with microscopic state `Z_t`, Markov dynamics `P(dz'|z)` (or an appropriate stochastic differential model), and an experimentally motivated candidate partition `Z=(A,E)` into subsystem and environment. Define physically instantiated internal variables `M` (memory/regulator), `Y` (sensing), `U` (actuation), and `C` (organizational constraints/capabilities), each recoverable from the physical state or coarse-graining, rather than introducing an external optimizer by definition.

An illustrative controlled diffusion (with mathematically admissible interventions) is

\[
 dX_t=f(X_t,C_t)dt+B(X_t,C_t)U_tdt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(X_t,C_t)dW_t,
\]
\[
 dM_t=g(M_t,Y_t,U_t,C_t)dt+\ldots,\qquad
 dC_t=h(C_t,X_t,M_t,U_t)dt+\ldots .
\]

Crucial distinction: `U_t` is an experimentally *intervenable* channel, but under unperturbed operation its value is generated by the internal physical organization. The existence of an intervention variable does **not** imply that a controller or a goal already exists.

The admissible set `Pi_phys(z,c)` depends on physical authority, energy availability, actuator topology, sensing, time and accessibility. The organization `C` can itself alter this set.

## 3. A causal possibility structure instead of a universal scalar

For horizon `T`, consider the family of path measures

\[
 \mathcal C_T(z,c)=\{P_{z,c}^{\pi}(d\omega):\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{phys}}(z,c)\}.
\]

`\omega` includes trajectories of the target, regulator and organizational state, as needed. This is *not* generally a set of equiprobable paths; it is a family of possible probability laws.

Distinguish independent **axes**; their exact metrics are context-dependent:

| Axis | Example mathematical object | Fundamental limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Future diversity | `H(X_T)` or path entropy under specified baseline | Can be high with zero actuation |
| Causal controllability | diameter of `{P^pi}` in total variation, or a reachable set | Can be high without good observation |
| Sensorimotor empowerment | `sup_{p(U)} I(U;Y_T)` | Depends on action/observation channel and horizon |
| Identifiability | separability of candidate transition models under designed interventions | Passive trajectories can be non-identifying |
| Operational knowledge | representation quality for relevant interventional future kernels | Depends on intervention family and outcome class |
| Organizational viability | `Pr^pi(tau_V > T)` under physically defined failure conditions | No universal canonical viability set |
| Endogenous autonomy | causal self-maintenance/regeneration of sensing, actuation and organization | Not identifiable from target trajectory entropy alone |

Independence demonstrations:

- If `Y` is independent fair noise, `H(Y)=1 bit` and `I(U;Y)=0`: randomness without control.
- If `Y=U` with a fair binary input, `H(Y|U)=0` and `I(U;Y)=1 bit`: deterministic conditional evolution with control.
- A hidden, unobserved context can invert the action consequence: latent physical controllability need not be usable epistemic control.
- A controller can control a neighbor while consuming the energy needed for its own continued operation.

**Structural lesson:** neither path entropy, nor predictive precision, nor empowerment, nor persistence alone is sufficient to classify agency.

## 4. Causal knowledge: exact and approximate

Let `H_t` denote the relevant history, `M_t` its internally realized compressed representation, and `\Omega_T` future organizational/environmental trajectories. For a physically admissible intervention policy `\pi`, define a controlled future kernel

\[
 K_T(h,\pi):=P(\Omega_T\in\cdot\mid H_t=h,\mathrm{do}(\pi)).
\]

**Exact causal sufficiency** on a specified family `\Pi`:

\[
 M\text{ is sufficient if }K_T(h,\pi)=\widehat K_T(m,\pi)
 \quad\forall h,m\text{ with }q(m|h)>0,\ \forall \pi\in\Pi.
\]

Equivalently, histories may share the same representation if they are indistinguishable with respect to the entire controlled future response class. This resembles input–output causal-state constructions and should not be presented as new.

A robust approximate distortion:

\[
 d_T(h,m)=\sup_{\pi\in\Pi_{\mathrm{phys}}}
 \mathrm{TV}\!\left(K_T(h,\pi),\widehat K_T(m,\pi)\right).
\]

Candidate **causal knowledge rate** (working terminology, not novelty claim):

\[
 R_{\mathrm{CK}}(\delta,T)=\inf_{q(m|h),\widehat K}
 I(H;M)\quad\text{subject to}\quad
 \mathbb E[d_T(H,M)]\le\delta.
\]

Compared with ordinary reward-based rate–distortion, the distortion is defined on an entire *family of counterfactual trajectory laws*, not one predetermined reward; it can later be projected into many admissible utility/viability queries. The difference from epsilon-transducers, predictive rate distortion, and robust control abstraction is **unverified** and requires careful analysis.

**Simple guarantee (standard, not original).** If for every feasible `(h,m)` and policy `\pi` we have `TV(K_T(h,pi),Khat_T(m,pi)) <= delta`, then any payoff `G(\omega) in [0,1]` has prediction error at most `delta` for each policy. Choosing a policy by optimizing the approximate model creates performance regret at most `2 delta` relative to the history-informed optimum, when both compare over the same admissible policies. Proof: add and subtract the approximate values of the two policies and use the optimality of the approximate decision.

This bound suggests the useful *generality* of a controlled-kernel representation: one representation can provide a guarantee for many possible reward functions. But these inequalities are standard properties of total variation and decision theory, not new theorems.

## 5. The least-action / information / control bridge

**Warning:** classical Hamilton's principle is an action-*stationarity* principle, `\delta S=0`, not a universal minimization principle. Coupled subsystems do not independently optimize their actions; interactions affect the total dynamics.

For nonlinear small-noise diffusion

\[
 dX_t=f(X_t)dt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(X_t)dW_t,
\]

Freidlin–Wentzell action under standard nondegeneracy assumptions is

\[
 S_T[\phi]=\tfrac12\int_0^T
 (\dot\phi-f(\phi))^{\!\top}
 (\sigma\sigma^\top)^{-1}(\dot\phi-f(\phi))dt.
\]

Rare trajectory probability scales as `exp(-S/epsilon)` in the appropriate large-deviation sense; quasipotentials are minimum actions over connecting paths and time. They quantify metastability and the barriers separating basins, even for nongradient systems.

For *matched* actuation and noise channels, with nondegenerate `\sigma`, identical initial laws, and conditions ensuring Girsanov's theorem,

\[
 dX_t=(f(X_t)+\sigma(X_t)u_t)dt+
 \sqrt{\varepsilon}\,\sigma(X_t)dW_t
\]

implies

\[
 D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P^u\Vert P^0)=
 \frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\mathbb E_{P^u}\int_0^T\|u_t\|^2dt.
\]

This equality measures the change in *path law* in information units through physical control effort. It is not an identity between semantic knowledge and energy, and it is not universal when controls act outside noisy directions, actuator constraints apply, or stochastic laws are singular.

The Gibbs/Boué–Dupuis variational relationship offers a complementary description:

\[
 -\lambda\log\mathbb E_{P^0}[e^{-G/\lambda}]
 =\inf_{Q\ll P^0}\{\mathbb E_Q[G]+\lambda D_{KL}(Q\Vert P^0)\}.
\]

Only a subset of `Q` may be physically reachable by the actual controller. **CDKA should focus on that gap** between mathematically possible path laws, physically realizable ones, and those that the subsystem can select *using its own internally maintained information*.

For coupled components with appropriate variational descriptions:

\[
 S_{\mathrm{total}}=\sum_i S_i+\sum_{i<j} S_{ij}+\ldots.
\]

The `S_ij` are interaction terms, so no independent optimization by components follows. Dissipative systems require appropriate stochastic and nonequilibrium frameworks rather than treating every process as a mechanical least-action problem.

## 6. Minimal agency: intervention-based criteria

Candidate *tests* (not claimed necessary-and-sufficient universal axioms):

1. **Self-individuation / organization:** some constituents, boundaries or constraints are produced or repaired by the organization itself.
2. **Interactional asymmetry:** the subsystem can causally modulate a distinguishable set of environmental consequences through its own channels.
3. **Information-conditioned intervention:** manipulating internal sensed/remembered information changes action selection while other physical resources are approximately matched.
4. **Functional regulation:** those interventions help sustain physically specified conditions necessary for continuing operation, relative to matched controls and disturbances.
5. **Renewal of the control relation:** sensing, actuation, and organizational constraints are themselves maintained or recovered over time by coupled internal dynamics.

A crystal has persistence but need not satisfy (2)–(5). A flame can be self-maintaining but need not possess information-conditioned selectable intervention. A passive coupled oscillator can entrain another without knowing/choosing among consequences. A thermostat has feedback but externally specified setpoint and an externally sustained organization. A bacterium could possess minimal agency without explicit symbolic prediction. The classification remains **relative to system boundaries, time scales, and physically testable causal channels**.

**Observation-only impossibility (elementary).** Construct two architectures yielding identical observed target law `P_A(X_0:T)=P_B(X_0:T)`; one generates its controls through an internal closed loop, the other through an external source that reproduces the same conditional action law. Any scalar functional of `P(X_0:T)` assigns them the same value. Intervention on the internal memory/coupling may distinguish them. Thus target-only dynamical entropy/complexity cannot generally identify endogenous autonomy.

**Avoid circularity:** do not replace an externally selected reward with a declared 'intrinsic' viability function and assume the teleology problem solved. Operational organizational constraints must be supported by observable self-production/maintenance dynamics; the choice of subsystem boundary remains a conceptual issue.

## 7. Viable-action topology and epistemic bifurcations

For contexts `w in W` and actions `a in A`, let `A_viable(w)` be the actions that satisfy an externally stated test of keeping a physically specified organization operating over a horizon. Define `W_a={w:a in A_viable(w)}`.

For a *single-step*, perfectly observed, deterministic, zero-failure decision problem with no other restrictions, the smallest internal memory alphabet enabling a viable action in every context has cardinality

\[
 k_* = \min\{|B|: B\subseteq A,\ \bigcup_{a\in B}W_a=W\}.
\]

This is a set-cover equivalence and is directly anticipated by Murray (2026), as well as broader minimum-information decision literatures. It gives a worst-case fixed-length storage requirement `ceil(log2 k_*)` bits, **not** necessarily a Shannon mutual-information lower bound equal to `log2 k_*`.

**Epistemic bifurcation example.** Suppose `W={w_L,w_R}` and both states are equally likely. Before a dynamical transition, `A_viable(w_L)={L,R}` and `A_viable(w_R)={L}`: zero bits suffice. After a change in coupled dynamics, `A_viable(w_L)={L}` and `A_viable(w_R)={R}`: exact viable control requires one bit. Environment state entropy stays at one bit in both cases, but the minimum *action-relevant* information changes.

**Open extension:** what happens when `A_viable`, sensors, actuators and even the organizational definition of viable operation evolve endogenously? Can dynamical bifurcations of physical attractors induce discontinuities or phase transitions in `R_CK` or robust viability? Which changes arise from altered geometry alone, and which require truly changed controllability, sensing, or operational organization?

### 7.1. Replicator dynamics as an evolving distribution of control mechanisms

Let `p_i` denote the fraction of physically instantiated regulatory mechanisms, policies, or reaction types `i`. Their relative amplification can follow

\[
 \dot p_i=p_i(F_i(x,c,p)-\bar F(x,c,p)),\qquad \bar F=\sum_i p_iF_i.
\]

With an independently fixed potential `\Phi(p)` satisfying `F_i=\partial_i\Phi`, the replicator flow is a natural gradient in Fisher–Shahshahani geometry. In the interior of the simplex,

\[
 \frac{d\Phi}{dt}=\sum_i p_i(F_i-\bar F)^2=\operatorname{Var}_p(F)\ge0.
\]

This monotonicity is **conditional** on the potential structure. Coupling the environment and organizational state back into selection,

\[
 \dot x=f(x,p,c),\qquad \dot c=g(c,x,p),
\]

creates additional terms `\nabla_x\Phi\cdot\dot x+\nabla_c\Phi\cdot\dot c` in `dPhi/dt` when the potential depends on `(x,c)`. Consequently a simple population-level Lyapunov argument need not survive coupling. Replicator–mutator systems with environment feedback are already known to undergo Hopf bifurcations and limit cycles; these phenomena are *precedents*, not CDKA discoveries.

The specific CDKA opportunity is not to call selection itself 'knowledge'. Instead ask whether changing `p` changes the **physically available intervention family** `Pi_phys(x,p,c)`, the topology of viable actions, and the amount of information needed for selective regulation. Frequencies `p` might physically encode past exposure (distributed memory); this encoding becomes operational knowledge only if causal intervention on that information changes future effective regulation. Interpret `F_i` as empirically identified growth or persistence rates in the physical model, not an unquestioned externally assigned utility.

Literature: [natural gradient ascent in evolutionary games (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biosystems.2024.105127); [limit cycles in replicator–mutator dynamics with game-environment feedback (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ifacol.2020.12.955).

## 8. Initial coupled-oscillator testbed (proposed, no results yet)

**Physical core:** two phase oscillators and a bistable noisy mode:

\[
 \dot\theta_1=\omega_1+K_{12}(C,M)\sin(\theta_2-\theta_1),
 \quad\dot\theta_2=\omega_2+K_{21}\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2),
\]
\[
 dq_t=(q_t-q_t^3+\kappa u_t)dt+\sqrt{2D}\,dW_t.
\]

For constant `u`, the potential is `U(q;u)=q^4/4-q^2/2-kappa*u*q`; bistability disappears at the saddle-node threshold `|kappa*u|=2/(3 sqrt(3))` under this normalization. A slow internal variable `M` modulates coupling or the actuator based on observations; a physically modeled resource variable `E` limits and potentially regenerates available sensing and actuation. Choose resources and perturbation couplings before comparisons; do not infer their ontology by fiat.

**Compare:**

- C0: passive couplings, no adaptable memory.
- C1: energy-matched reactive feedback.
- C2: persistent internal memory, without planning.
- C3: memory and models that discriminate intervention-dependent transitions.
- C4: external controller/replay matched to C3 on observed trajectories (causal provenance control).
- C5: lesioned/scrambled memory, preserving its marginal statistics where possible.

**Report:** empirical attractors/phase locking and basin maps; minimum action and quasipotential barriers; identifiable action consequences; path-distribution diameters; empowerment; causal-kernel distortion; memory capacity and regret; persistence time and resource cost; self-repair of sensors/actuators/constraints. Compare under fixed energy, noise, horizon and observation budgets. Test for *sufficient* positive evidence of operational agency, not a metaphysical threshold.

**Key negative controls:** an external oracle producing the same behavior; purely dissipative persistence; an actuator with no informative sensing; memory that predicts states but is not useful for control; powerful control that destroys its own future resources.

## 9. Research hypotheses, risk and proof targets

### H1 — Controlled-kernel sufficiency beyond fixed rewards

Representations sufficient for a family of controlled future measures yield uniform control guarantees across multiple downstream viability/reward probes. **Status:** much follows from known stochastic process and decision-theoretic results; unique contribution not established. Aim: characterize minimality, tractability, and changes when the admissible intervention family evolves.

### H2 — Endogenous epistemic bifurcation

Physical bifurcations and altered organizational constraints can change the minimum information needed for robust viable intervention, even when state entropy and sensor alphabet do not change. **Status:** static example established; sequential/endogenous extension open. Look for a nontrivial theorem with explicit regularity conditions, not just a change in labels for action admissibility.

### H3 — Path action and informational cost under organization-changing interventions

Explore conditions in which transition barrier changes, path-space KL costs and information constraints can be bounded together across interacting subsystems. **Status:** existing Girsanov/FW/Schrödinger-bridge machinery; new results would require genuinely structured resource/organization constraints or novel inequalities.

### H4 — Causal criteria for endogenous autonomy

Operational autonomy cannot be inferred from the target's path law alone and requires interventions on sensing, regulating and self-producing organization. **Status:** elementary observational-equivalence argument; important conceptual constraint, not new mathematically. Aim: formal equivalence classes of systems under structural interventions and discriminative tests for minimal agency.

### H5 — No universal scalar should be assumed

The family of future measures, their geometry and their resource-constrained realization may be fundamentally richer than any one entropy, reward, or complexity score. **Status:** a methodological stance to turn into counterexamples and possible impossibility results, not an established universal theorem.

## 10. Priority sequence

**Phase 1 — Definitions and negative examples.** Reconcile the model with Polani/Fox/Tishby, epsilon-transducers, Kolchinsky/Wolpert, Murray, Barandiaran, Beni. Identify required physical input and what can actually be measured.

**Phase 2 — Formal results.** Prove general `TV -> regret` baseline and simple cardinality bounds; identify a mathematically nontrivial result involving *evolving* viability/action geometry and information rates. Investigate the appropriate notion of control costs using path laws and resource dynamics.

**Phase 3 — Minimal simulation.** Build reproducible coupled oscillator / bistable noisy plant; use matched-resource controls and causal ablations. First demonstrate independence of diversity, causality, knowledge and persistence before seeking emergent agency.

**Phase 4 — Theory revision.** Seek counterexamples that satisfy candidate agency criteria without intuitively possessing agency; revise the primitives rather than defending an overbroad interpretation. Only then consider a position paper or theorem paper.

## 11. Questions unresolved

- Can endogenous normative constraints be defined without presupposing organismal or architectural individuality?
- How does one determine subsystem boundaries from physics rather than an observer's purpose? Is complete observer invariance possible or necessary?
- Is prospective counterfactual modelling essential to agency, or can minimal biochemical regulation instantiate it without an explicit internal forward model?
- Can resource-constrained physically feasible intervention sets be inferred rather than stipulated?
- What separates mere influence, usable control, autonomous selection and the maintenance of capacity for future influence?
- Can composing stochastic actions of coupled subsystems create genuinely new reachable path-law structures, rather than merely expanding a state description?
- When do informational minima change continuously, discontinuously, or with a robust control threshold as attractor basins change?
