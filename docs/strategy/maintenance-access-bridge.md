# Maintenance-access bridge: one integrated result for R11/R14 and F7

**4 November 2026. Base: CDKA v15, commit `2eb1da7b635da2165e3b767440371a69583df3d0`.**

## Decision and larger purpose

The target is constructive control over the distinctions that a continuation
requires. F7 asks for preservation relative to a task instead of identical
internal labels, and asks that the cost of discovering a relation be reported
separately from the cost of its final certificate. CDKA asks that the
maintenance selector itself be physically implemented and paid. This increment
connects those obligations in the inherited register model.

The useful comparison is an old library of physical continuations versus the
same library with one paid causal capability. A compression can be exact for
the old library and insufficient for the new one. The implementation supplies
both an enlarged interface and a specific continuation explaining the extra
distinction. Only R11/R14 remain active; this is an additive bridge, not a
new independent physical-model branch.

## Result that closes this increment

- The old response space is generated from DIFFUSE/REGENERATE and the final
  actual-voter event. It has dimension 10, including mass.
- One maintenance slot, selected by that same real six-NAND voter, requires
  dimension 12. The certificate respects at most one maintenance call.
- The old interface is reusable exactly when `M W0` is contained in `W0`.
  A failed containment yields a real separating continuation and two
  admissible initial laws; a successful extension gives checked coordinate
  transformations and a rank certificate.
- Resource zero and one require 10 and 12 coordinates respectively. The
  32-state process therefore admits a 22-coordinate linear evaluator,
  alongside a stronger 24-class Markov quotient.
- For at most two ordinary updates and an additional maintenance interval,
  the relevant ranks are 7 and 12. These are different horizon conventions
  from two total primitive calls.

The physical controller retains its three cells. The evaluator signature is
not handed to it, and it never observes the hidden substrate mode or the
repair outcome. The paid routine uses a current physical vote to request
repair; there is no reliable external diagnostic trigger.

## Discovery, checking and physical costs

For `n` explicit states, `a` old operators, `r0` old rank and `r1` extended
rank, the compiler considers `|G| + a*r0` old candidates, `r0` maintenance
images, and at most `a*(r1-r0)` old-prefix images of new columns. In the
fixture these are 22, 10 and 4 candidates: 36 total, or 14 after reuse of
the old closure. Only two new independent response columns are retained.

These are algebraic candidate counts, not constant-time operations. Applying
a sparse kernel costs its nonzero entries per column; rational elimination,
coefficient bit lengths and certificate serialization have additional cost.
The explicit 16-state physical kernels are inputs to this analysis. No bound
is claimed for compiling an arbitrary combinatorial world into them.

The checker replays supplied legal words and verifies rational coordinate
identities and inverse-minor identities. It does not run discovery. Raw
gate/repair/health enumeration checks physical-kernel correctness separately.

Installed gates, gate evaluations, service slots, cell writes and time are
separate resource quantities. The two-update example `D,M,R,readout` uses
the same six installed NANDs, 18 NAND evaluations, three writes, six cell
channel-update opportunities and one prepaid service slot. DIFFUSE is not
silently reinterpreted as a three-cell write. Only the service-slot resource
is a hard feasibility bound in this increment; the other quantities are
reported, not combined into an uncalibrated energy sum.

## Exactness versus significance

`M_s=M_0+s Delta`, and `M_0 W0` stays in `W0`. Thus every positive repair
success probability requires the same enlarged exact space, although its
effect on the displayed pair grows linearly with `s`. For the 24 words
containing at most two ordinary updates and one maintenance, a common
prediction for the two laws has exact minimax error half their maximum
response gap; the midpoint law attains it.

For tolerance 0.005 probability units per prediction, the displayed pair
can share that approximate prediction precisely up to repair success
0.6588667095447904. This is a certified statement for this pair and family,
not a universal approximation threshold over all physical preparations.

## Controls that change the interpretation

Exposing an intermediate voter result already enlarges the old response
space to 12. Therefore the main library explicitly returns no D/R or
maintenance observation before the final readout. Maintenance is one new
causal use of the voter, not the only conceivable way to distinguish the
two laws. Unconditional repair also separates them; conditioning is the
specific implemented routine studied here.

The compared laws are admissible initial preparations at a fixed boundary.
The result does not prove that both are reached from one fixed initial
sensor experiment. The objective is the readout event, not earlier
whole-plant survival, and the model does not repair every piece of its own
infrastructure indefinitely.

## Consequence for the next proof obligation

The completed result supplies the contract for introducing a new capability:
test its image against the existing response space, retain a separator when
it fails, and price the actual routine that realizes it. The next unresolved
question is whether a task-relative approximate interface can be compiled
under a shared physical budget without first constructing the full explicit
kernel. That is the place to connect F7's proposed relations and local
refutations to this certified maintenance extension.

Before enlarging the physical model, this existing result should be used as
a mandatory positive/negative fixture for that compiler. Exact old-library
equivalence, an affordable separating routine, the zero-success control and
the intermediate-observation control make its failures interpretable.
