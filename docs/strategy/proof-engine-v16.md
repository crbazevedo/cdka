## What was learned

A resource-feasible policy can still waste risk on maintenance of a healthy interpreter. A persistent diagnostic is not always current. Internal hardware dependence can make actions statistically depend on hidden health even when input bits are uninformative; that is not knowledge in the register. Operational recurrence does not remove the fixed fatal error in task execution.

## Refinement and remaining idealizations

The diagnostic sensor, resource guard, routing and installation interface are still calibrated/common primitives. No recurrent reliability claim is made for all infrastructure. The program is stationary with finite physical memory; it does not optimize arbitrary history-dependent or time-indexed policies.

The best immediate next proof target is policy-uniform regenerative realization under a specified acquisition grammar, followed by its smallest failure when data are retained instead of refreshed. R14 must compare costs of reading/refreshing/retaining on the SAME resource ledger before promoting a more elaborate controller. Do not claim a generally cheaper architecture from this fixed common operating bundle.

## Dead-end map

- Fresh-memory factorization fails under retention: R11/R13 retain the necessary dependence, using a separating history.
- Healthy maintenance is overused: compare conditional expected gain and resource continuation, not repair success alone.
- Exact simulator grows: first exploit emission closure or structural symmetry; measure total build-and-query cost against the raw oracle.
- Novelty is subsumed by finite-controller theory: retain this operationally closed benchmark/certificate and state the precise unresolved implementation/cost extension.
- Constant fatal hazard blocks indefinite viability: do not tune away the theorem. Change the recovery architecture and preserve the old result as a control.
